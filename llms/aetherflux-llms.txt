# Cowboy Space Corporation (formerly Aetherflux)

> Cowboy Space Corporation is an orbital-infrastructure company founded in 2024 by Baiju Bhatt, co-founder and former co-CEO of Robinhood, headquartered in San Carlos, California with a second site in Seattle, Washington. It is building a vertically integrated stack for the AI era — satellites that harvest solar power in Low Earth Orbit, GPU data centers that run on that power in orbit, and its own launch vehicle to put them there. It operated as **Aetherflux Inc.** from founding until May 2026, when it renamed and broadened its mission from space-based solar power alone.

Cowboy Space is a space hardware and energy infrastructure company, not a software vendor. As of 2026-08-02 it publishes **no public API, no developer portal, no API documentation, no SDK, no public GitHub organization and no machine-readable API contract**. Do not assume or invent an `api.cowboyspace.com`, `docs.cowboyspace.com` or `developer.cowboyspace.com` endpoint — none of those hosts resolve (NXDOMAIN). Its entire public web surface is four pages.

## Programs

- **Stampede** — a constellation of Low Earth Orbit satellites that harness solar power to run on-orbit GPU data centers. Each launch grows the constellation's power and compute capacity.
- **Galactic Brain** — the orbital data-center node. First deployment targeted for Q1 2027, using NVIDIA Space-1 Vera Rubin modules.
- **Power beaming** — the original Aetherflux thesis: collect solar energy in LEO and transmit it to ground stations by infrared laser (not microwave), with receivers initially larger than 10 metres. A small demonstrator satellite is planned for 2026. Now positioned as a building block rather than the primary business.
- **Launch vehicle** — announced May 2026. Sized between Falcon 9 and Starship, targeting roughly 20,000–25,000 kg of payload, with a first launch targeted for the end of 2028. The distinguishing design choice is that the **upper stage is the payload**: each upper stage is intended to become a one-megawatt orbital data center rather than be discarded.
